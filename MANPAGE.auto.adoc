== NAME

slopmid - a piano roll and clip arranger that handles MIDI channels for you

== SYNOPSIS

*slopmid* [_FILE_.smm]

*slopmid* *-h* | *--help*

== DESCRIPTION

slopmid is a pattern-based MIDI sequencer that plays through the built-in *slopgs* GS synthesizer.
You write *clips*, which are loops made of *tracks*. Each track has one sound (a bank and program,
or a drum kit). Then you place clips on the 32 *lanes* of the *Song* window to build an
arrangement.

You don't have to pick a MIDI channel. While the song plays, each track gets a channel only while
one of its notes is sounding, and slopmid hands out the lowest free channel. One track can
therefore share a channel with many others over a song, and more tracks than channels can exist as
long as no more than 16 sound at the same moment. Drum tracks get the drum channel, plus extra
drum parts when you need them. If you do want a melodic track on one particular channel, you can
lock it to that channel. See *PLAYBACK AND CHANNEL ALLOCATION*.

The timing is fixed at 96 ticks per beat in 4/4 time.
// src: src/model.nim:6-7

== OPTIONS

_FILE_.smm::
  Open this song at startup. If it can't be read, a *File error* popup shows the reason and
  slopmid starts with an empty song.

*-h*, *--help*::
  Print the usage line and exit.

== TUTORIAL

This walkthrough builds a short song: a drum loop, a bass line and a bent lead. You then arrange
them and export the result. Each step names only the controls it uses; the reference sections
below cover every control in detail.

=== 1. Get oriented

Start *slopmid* with no arguments. The window has four parts:

* the *transport bar* under the menu: Play/Pause, Stop, Metronome, BPM, Snap, the position, the
  Song/Clip mode switch and the channel meter;
* *Clips* (top left): the current clip and its tracks;
* *Song* (bottom left): the arrangement lanes;
* *Piano roll* (right): the notes of the focused track.

The piano roll is greyed out because no track has focus yet.

=== 2. A drum track

. In *Clips*, click *ADD* (under the track table). A track row appears.
. Open the row's sound combo, which reads `000:000 Acoustic Grand Piano`. Scroll to the bottom and
  pick *Rhythm*. The row now reads `Rhythm 000`.
. Click the black preview strip on the right of the row. It turns blue-grey and the piano roll
  becomes active for this track. The key column now shows General MIDI drum names such as
  `Kick`, `Snare` and `ClHat`.
. The roll opens around C6. Scroll down with the mouse wheel to the `Kick` row (key 36).
. Set *Snap* in the transport to `1/4`.
. Make sure the *Draw* tool (the pencil, top-left corner of the piano roll) is selected, then
  left-click on the `Kick` row at beats 1, 2, 3 and 4 of bar 1. Each click adds a note, one beat
  long, and plays it.
. Set *Snap* to `1/8`. Click once on the `ClHat` row, then drag that note's right edge in to an
  eighth. New notes copy the length of the last note you clicked or resized, so the rest of the
  hats you click in at every eighth will be eighths too. If notes of the same pitch overlap, the
  overlap is drawn red.
. Press *Space*. The clip loops; its loop point is the orange mark on the ruler, at the end of the
  last bar that has a note.
. Press *Space* again to pause.

To remove a note, right-click it. Hold the right button and sweep to erase several.

=== 3. A bass track

. In *Clips*, click *ADD* again. Set the new track's sound to `000:038 Synth Bass 1`, or type
  `38` in its *P#* field.
. Click its preview strip to focus it.
. Click a key in the key column to hear the pitch. Clicking further right on a key plays it
  louder.
. Draw notes. Like the hats, each new note copies the length and velocity of the last note you
  clicked or resized.
. Drag a note to move it. Dragging across pitches plays each pitch as you go.
. Make the bass wider than the drums by drawing into bar 2. The loop point moves to the end of
  bar 2 for the whole clip.

=== 4. Velocity

The strip under the notes is the *Vel* lane. Each note has a stem there.

* Drag up or down across stems to set velocities. The tooltip shows the value, for example
  `100 - 79%`.
* Select notes first (step 5 below) to have the drag change only those notes.
* Drag the border between the sheet and the lane to resize it.

=== 5. Selecting and editing groups

. Click the *Select* tool (the dashed box next to the pencil).
. Drag a box around some notes. Shift+drag adds to the selection.
. Drag any selected note: the whole group moves together.
. Try the following:
  ** *Shift+Up* / *Shift+Down* to transpose by a semitone, *Ctrl+Up* / *Ctrl+Down* by an octave;
  ** *Ctrl+D* to duplicate the selection right after itself;
  ** *Ctrl+Q* to quantize the selection to the snap;
  ** *Ctrl+A* to select all, and *Ctrl+A* again to select none.
. *Ctrl+Z* undoes; *Ctrl+Shift+Z* or *Ctrl+Y* redoes.

=== 6. A lead with pitch bend and pan

. Add a third track. Set it to `000:081 Lead 2 (sawtooth)` and focus it.
. Draw a few long notes.
. Choose the *Bend* tool (the swoop, the third corner button). Every note now shows a white line
  with a point at each end.
. Click the line of a note partway along to add a bend point, then drag it up. The note bends
  upward. Bend runs up to 24 semitones either way.
. *Ctrl+click* a point to send it back to the note's own pitch. Right-drag over a middle point to
  delete it.
. Switch the lane combo (below the key column, showing `Vel`) to *Pan*. With *Draw*, click to add
  pan points; the white bar marks the centre. *Ctrl+click* a point to centre it.
. Do the same in *Vol* (CC 7) or *Exp* (CC 11) mode if you like. The small combo under the mode
  sets how often the curve is sent: `1/16` to `1/96`.

=== 7. A second clip

. Name the clip by typing `verse` into *Clip name*.
. Click *Duplicate*. A copy, also named `verse`, becomes the current clip. Rename it `chorus`.
. Change some notes in `chorus`.
. Use the *Clip* combo to switch between the two clips.

=== 8. Arrange

. Select `verse` in the *Clip* combo.
. In *Song*, with the *Draw* tool, click in lane 1 at bar 1. A `verse` box appears, as long as the
  clip's loop. Keep holding the button and drag to position it before you let go.
. Pick `chorus` in the *Clip* combo and place it after `verse`.
. Drag a box's right edge past its end to repeat the clip. White lines mark each repeat. Drag the
  left edge to start the clip part-way in.
. Switch the transport to *Song* mode and press *Space*. The song plays from the song playhead
  and stops, rewinding to bar 1, at the end of the last box.
. Click or drag on the Song window's ruler to move the song playhead.

The *M* and *S* boxes at the left of each lane mute or solo the lane. They appear only when the
lanes are tall enough; Ctrl+middle-drag zooms the lanes vertically.

=== 9. Save and export

. *Ctrl+S* saves. The first save asks for a file name and adds `.smm` if you leave it off. The
  title bar shows the name, with `*` while there are unsaved changes.
. *Ctrl+E* (*File > Export MIDI...*) writes a Standard MIDI File of what you'd hear in the current
  mode: the arrangement in Song mode, one pass of the clip in Clip mode.
. *File > Export WAV...* renders the same thing to a WAV file, with a progress bar.

Quit with *File > Exit*, which asks about unsaved changes. Closing the window with the window
manager's close button quits at once *without asking*.

The menu bar holds *File*, *Edit* and *Window*. Below it, the transport bar stays fixed so no

=== Transport bar

*Play* / *Pause*, *Space*::
  Toggles playback. In Clip mode it does nothing until the current clip has a track. Space is
  ignored while you are typing in a text field.
*Stop*::
  Stops and returns the playhead of the current mode to the start.
*Metronome*::
  Toggles a click on every beat, with a higher click on each bar's first beat. The click is
  heard live only; it is never exported.
*BPM*::
  The tempo, from 20 to 999. Use the +/- buttons or type a value.
*Snap*::
  The grid that drawing, moving, resizing, pasting and quantizing use. See *SNAP VALUES*.
Position::
  _bar_:_beat_:_tick_ of the playing mode's playhead. Ticks run from 00 to 95.
*Song* / *Clip*::
  Selects what plays. *Clip* loops the current clip. *Song* plays the placements in the Song
  window. Switching modes stops playback. Each mode keeps its own playhead.
`Rhy:a/b Mldy:c/d`::
  Channels in use right now: _a_ of the _b_ channels reserved for drums, and _c_ of the _d_
  melodic channels. _b_ + _d_ is always 16.
// src: src/slopmid.nim:6-46, src/player.nim:80-88

== CLIPS WINDOW

The top row controls the current clip:

*Clip* combo::
  Picks the current clip. The current clip is the one that plays in Clip mode, the one the piano
  roll edits, and the one the Song window's Draw tool places. Switching clips stops Clip-mode
  playback, clears focus, and clears the old clip's mutes and solos.
*Add*::
  Appends an empty clip and makes it current.
*Remove*::
  Deletes the current clip and every placement of it. This button does nothing when only one clip
  exists. Undo brings the clip back.
*Duplicate*::
  Appends a copy of the current clip, with its name, and makes the copy current.
*Clip name*::
  Up to 63 characters. A clip with no name is shown as `Clip` _N_.

The timeline above the tracks shows the bars of the clip's loop. Click or drag on it to set the
clip playhead.

Each track row contains:

*X*::
  Deletes the track. This can be undone.
*M* / *S*::
  Mute and solo. When any track is soloed, only the soloed tracks play. Mutes and solos are not
  saved, are not undone, and are cleared when the clip changes. They apply in both modes.
Sound combo::
  The 128 General MIDI programs as _bank_:_program_ _name_, plus *Rhythm*, which turns the track
  into a drum track.
*Bk#*::
  The bank, from 0 to 127, sent as Bank Select (CC 0) before the program change. Greyed out on
  drum tracks.
*P#*::
  The program, from 0 to 127. On a drum track, this program number is sent on the drum channel.
*Lock*::
  A padlock button. An open padlock means the track is not locked. Click it to lock the track to
  channel 0, and a channel field appears beside it. Click the padlock again to unlock the track; the
  channel is forgotten. The field sets the channel from 0 to 15, with its *-* and *+* buttons or by
  typing. It stops at 0 and 15 and never unlocks the track. Channels count from 0, so channel 10,
  the GM drum channel, is 9. New tracks are unlocked. Greyed out on drum tracks, which ignore their
  lock. See *Locked channels*.
Preview strip::
  The track's notes drawn as small bars, with the playhead lines. Click the strip to focus the
  track in the piano roll. The focused track's strip is blue-grey.

*ADD*, under the table, appends a new track: program 0, bank 0, melodic, not locked.

== PIANO ROLL

The piano roll edits the focused track. It is greyed out until a track has focus.

=== Layout

The tool buttons sit in the top-left corner: *Draw*, *Select* and *Bend*. The ruler runs along the
top, the key column on the left, the note sheet in the middle and the lane at the bottom. There
are scrollbars on the right and bottom.

* *Ruler*: click or drag to set the clip playhead. Pasted notes land there. Bar numbers are drawn
  on it, and an orange mark shows the clip's loop point.
* *Key column*: note names, C-1 to G9. On a drum track, keys 35 to 81 show abbreviated GM drum
  names. Click a key to hear it; the further right you click on the key, the higher the velocity.
* *Sheet*: the notes. A note's brightness follows its velocity, and selected notes are yellow with
  a white outline. When there is room, a note shows its name. Where two notes of the same pitch
  overlap, the overlapping part is drawn bright red.
* *Lane*: the *Vel*, *Vol*, *Exp* or *Pan* editor. Drag the border above it to resize it (10% to
  90% of the height).

In Song mode, the playhead shows as one line per placement of this clip that is playing. A fainter
line marks the clip's own playhead, which is where pastes go.
// src: src/pianoroll.nim:65-110, 462-583

=== Draw tool

* Left-click on empty space to add a note at the clicked pitch, starting at the snap line at or
  before the mouse. The new note copies the length and velocity of the last note you clicked or
  dragged. Keep holding to drag the new note into place.
* Drag a note to move it, snapped. If the note is selected, the whole selection moves with it.
* Drag the right edge of a note (the last 6 pixels) to resize it. A selected note resizes the whole
  selection by the same amount. No note gets shorter than one snap.
* Right-click a note to delete it. Hold the right button and sweep to delete several.
* Placing or dragging a note plays it.

=== Select tool

* Drag on empty space to draw a box. On release, every note the box touches is selected, and all
  others are deselected. Hold *Shift* to add to the selection instead.
* Click a note to select only that note. *Shift+click* adds it to the selection. Clicking a note
  that is already selected keeps the current selection, so you can drag the group.
* Moving and resizing work as with the Draw tool.
* Right-click does not delete in this tool.
// src: src/pianoroll.nim:292-339, src/edit.nim:62-84, 295-315

=== Bend tool

Each note has its own pitch bend line, drawn from its start to its end. In this tool the notes
themselves can't be edited.

* Every note shows two end points, which can move only up and down, and any number of middle
  points.
* The vertical distance from the note's row is the bend in semitones. It is continuous, limited
  to plus or minus 24. Horizontally, points move by the snap.
* Click a note's line, or its body, to add a point at the snap line at that height.
  *Ctrl+click* adds the point at zero bend.
* Drag a point to move it. If the point is selected, the whole selection moves with it.
* *Ctrl+click* a point to set it to zero bend. If it is selected, the whole selection goes to zero.
* Right-click, or right-drag across, middle points to delete them. End points can't be deleted.
* *Shift+click* adds a point to the selection. Drag on empty space to select points with a box.
  *Ctrl+A* selects all points, or none. *Delete* removes the selected middle points.

A track has one pitch wheel. When bent notes overlap on the same track, the note that started last
controls the wheel. Lines and points that lose the wheel to another note are drawn black. To bend
overlapping notes separately, put them on separate tracks.

Note: selecting the Bend tool gives every note of the focused track its two flat end points. That
counts as an edit, so it marks the song as changed and adds an undo step.
// src: src/model.nim:13-14, 84-128, src/edit.nim:88-142, src/pianoroll.nim:111-115, 211-290, 535-550

=== Vel lane

Every note has a stem at its start, with a faint line along its length at its velocity's height.

* Press on a stem, or on the faint line, and drag up or down to set the velocity, from 1 to 127.
  While you drag, every stem the mouse crosses is set too.
* If any notes are selected, the drag affects only selected notes.
* Hover to see a tooltip listing each note under the mouse, for example `C4: 100 - 79%`.
// src: src/pianoroll.nim:360-363, 423-434, 437-460

=== Vol, Exp and Pan lanes

Each track has three curves: *Vol* (CC 7, default 100), *Exp* (CC 11, default 127) and *Pan* (CC
10, default 64, the centre). A curve ramps in straight lines between its points. It holds the
first point's value before that point and the last point's value up to the loop point.

* *Draw* tool: click on empty space to add a point at the snap line and drag it. Drag a point to
  move it. Right-click or right-drag over points to delete them; the last point can't be deleted.
* *Select* tool: click a point to select it (*Shift* adds), drag on empty space to box-select, and
  drag a selected point to move the selection.
* *Pan* only: *Ctrl+click* a point to centre it, or *Ctrl+click* the curve to add a centred point.
  A white bar marks the centre. The tooltip reads `64 - Center`, `0 - 100% L` or `127 - 100% R`.
* Points can't pass their neighbours, can't go before tick 0, and can't go past the loop point.
* The resolution combo (`1/16`, `1/32`, `1/64` or `1/96`) sets how often a sloped curve is sent
  while playing. It is saved per track. The default is `1/96`.

A curve with no points is drawn as a single point at its default value. Opening the lane adds that
point to the track, which counts as an edit.
// src: src/model.nim:11-15, 71-82, src/edit.nim:146-183, src/pianoroll.nim:364-422

== SONG WINDOW

The Song window has 32 lanes. Each box in a lane is a *placement*: a clip played from a start time
for a length. Placements in the same lane can't overlap. Each box is coloured by its clip and
shows its clip name and a miniature of the clip's notes. White lines inside a box mark where the
clip starts over.

*Draw* tool::
  Click empty space to place the current clip, starting at the snap line at or before the mouse.
  The new placement is as long as the clip's loop, or shorter if the next placement in the lane
  is closer. Keep holding to drag it into place. Right-click a placement to remove it.
*Select* tool::
  Drag a box to select placements. *Shift* adds to the selection. Click a placement to select it.
Either tool::
  * Click a placement to make its clip the current clip. In Song mode playback keeps going.
  * Drag a placement to move it between times and lanes. If it is selected, the selection moves
    with it.
  * Drag a placement's right edge to change its length. Past the clip's loop, the clip repeats.
    The right edges of all selected placements move by the same amount.
  * Drag a placement's left edge to trim the start, so the clip begins part-way in. This moves
    only the placement you grab.
  * While a drag would overlap another placement, the moving placements turn red. Releasing there
    puts them back where they were.
Ruler::
  Click or drag to set the song playhead.
Lane headers::
  The lane number, *M* (mute) and *S* (solo). The headers are hidden when the lanes are shorter
  than 22 pixels.

Song mode plays from the song playhead to the end of the last placement, then stops and rewinds to
the start.

== PLAYBACK AND CHANNEL ALLOCATION

Each note asks for a channel when it starts:

. If its track is locked to a channel (and is not a drum track), the note uses that channel.
. Otherwise, if its track already has a note sounding, the note uses that track's channel.
. Otherwise a melodic track takes the lowest free channel that isn't reserved for drums. A drum
  track takes the first free drum channel, or shares the last drum channel if all are busy.
. If the channel last played a different bank or program, slopmid sends a bank select and program
  change first. It also sends the track's Vol, Exp and Pan values and its bend, if they differ
  from what the channel has.
. When no channel is free, the note is dropped. A locked note is never dropped.

A channel becomes free when its last note ends.

*Drum channels.* Channel 10 is always a drum channel. When playback starts, slopmid counts the
most drum tracks that can sound at once. In Song mode, that is the largest total across
overlapping audible placements. For each drum track beyond the first, it reserves one more drum
channel, in the order 16, 15, 14, 13, 12, 11, turning each into a drum part with the GS *USE FOR
RHYTHM PART* message. Each reserved channel leaves one fewer channel for melodic tracks.

The reservation is fixed while playback runs, because the GS message cuts every sounding note. If
you add a drum track during playback, it shares a drum channel until you pause and play again.
// src: src/player.nim:6-7, 48-78, 99-135, 260-291, 299-333

*Locked channels.* A lock overrides the rules above, and it is not exclusive. Any other track can
use the locked channel while the locked track has no note sounding on it, and a locked note starts
on its channel even when another track's note already sounds there. Then:

* Both tracks play on that channel. A bank, program, curve or bend message that one track sends
  applies to the other track's notes too, because these messages are per channel.
* If both tracks play the same pitch, a note off from one can end the other's note, because a MIDI
  note off names only a channel and a pitch.
* An unlocked track skips a channel while a locked note sounds on it. When a locked and an unlocked
  track start on the same tick, the locked one goes first, whatever the track order, so the
  unlocked one takes the next free channel.
* Locking a melodic track to channel 10 (9), or to a channel reserved as a drum part, makes it play
  drum sounds.
// src: src/model.nim:38, src/player.nim:24-26, 280, 299-308

*Pitch bend.* The bend range of every channel is set to 24 semitones (RPN 0) the first time that
channel bends. The wheel returns to centre when the channel's last note ends.
// src: src/model.nim:13, src/player.nim:229-236, 258-259

*Curves.* A track's curves are sent only on the channels it is sounding on, at most once per
resolution step and only when the value changes. A track sends a curve only if that curve has
points.
// src: src/player.nim:187-196, 241-251

*Previews.* Clicked keys and dragged notes play on a second synth instance, so previews never take
a channel from the song.
// src: src/audio.nim:20, src/model.nim:9, src/pianoroll.nim:341-358

*Audio.* slopgs renders 16-bit stereo at 22050 Hz in blocks of 1024 frames through SDL2. Its sounds
come from a gm.dls built into the program.
// src: src/wrap/slopgs.nim:19-21, 35-49, src/audio.nim:59-64

When there are unsaved changes, New, Open and Exit ask *Save changes?* with *Save*, *Don't save*
and *Cancel*. If you choose *Save* for a song that has no file name yet, you pick a name first. The
file dialogs add `.smm`, `.mid` or `.wav` when the name doesn't end with it. The file shortcuts
don't work while a popup is open or while you are typing in a text field.

Opening a file clears the undo history.

=== The .smm format

An `.smm` file is plain text with one record per line. The first word of each line names the
record. Ticks are 96 per beat. Each `note`, `bend` and `cc` line belongs to the most recent `track`
line, and each `track` line belongs to the most recent `clip` line.

----
bpm <tempo>                              20 to 999
clip [<name>]                            the rest of the line is the name
track <bank> <program> <res> <rhythm> [<lock>]
                                         res: 0..3 = 1/16, 1/32, 1/64, 1/96; rhythm: 0 or 1;
                                         lock: -1 or 0..15, -1 if left out
note <start> <len> <pitch> <vel>         len > 0; pitch and vel 0..127
bend <t> <v> <t> <v> ...                 the previous note's bend points; v in semitones
cc <curve> <t> <v> ...                   curve: 0 vol, 1 exp, 2 pan
place <clip> <lane> <start> <len> <offset>   clip index from 0, lane 0..31
----

In a `bend` record, the first pair is the note's start and the last pair is its end; their `t`
values are ignored. The middle pairs count ticks from the note's start.

A file with no `clip` line, with an unknown record, with a track lock outside -1 to 15, or with a
placement of a clip that doesn't exist is rejected with a *File error*. Mutes, solos, selections
and the layout are not saved.
// src: src/smm.nim, src/model.nim:25, 38

=== MIDI export

The export is a type 1 Standard MIDI File at 96 ticks per beat. It contains exactly what playback
would send in the current mode: the arrangement in Song mode, or one pass of the current clip in
Clip mode. Muted tracks and lanes are left out, and the channel choices are the same as during
playback.

Track 1 holds the tempo and a 4/4 time signature. If extra drum channels are needed, it also holds
a GS reset and the rhythm-part messages at tick 0. Each channel that plays a note gets a track of
its own. Redundant controller messages are removed; the file sounds the same.
// src: src/smf.nim:21-107

=== WAV export

The WAV export covers the same material as the MIDI export. slopgs renders it with a fresh synth
instance on a background thread, faster than real time. The file is 16-bit stereo at 22050 Hz,
without the metronome, plus two seconds at the end for notes to fade out. A progress popup shows
while it renders.
// src: src/audio.nim:74-136

== UNDO

Each edit becomes one undo step when you release the mouse button or leave a text field. A whole
drag is therefore one step. Undo covers clips, tracks, notes, curves, bends and placements.

Undo does not cover the tempo, mutes, solos or selections. Undo or redo also empties the Song
window's clipboard.

== LIMITS AND QUIRKS

* 4/4 time only, and one tempo for the whole song.
* 32 lanes. At most 16 tracks can sound at once, because each sounding track holds one channel.
  Tracks locked to a channel can share it, so more than 16 can sound.
  Every reserved drum channel is a channel melodic tracks can't use. When every channel is busy,
  new notes on melodic tracks are dropped.
* A clip's loop point is the end of the last bar that has a note, and a clip is at least one bar
  long. Curve points can't be placed past the loop point.
* Closing the window through the window manager quits without asking about unsaved changes. Use
  *File > Exit* instead.
* The window layout and the open file are not remembered between runs.
* Opening the Bend tool, or a curve lane on a track without points, adds default points. This marks
  the song as changed.
* slopmid has no MIDI input or output ports and no MIDI import. Sound comes only from the built-in
  slopgs synth.

== BUILDING

You need Nim 2.2.10 or later (C++ backend), the SDL2 development files and the git submodules
(`deps/slopgs`, `deps/imgui-filebrowser`). Restore the Nim dependencies with `atlas rep`.

----
make linux      # ./slopmid, stripped
make windows    # slopmid.exe, cross-built with mingw; SDL2 linked statically
make            # both
----

The Windows build needs `SDL2_MINGW` set to the `x86_64-w64-mingw32` directory of the SDL2 mingw
development package. Build with `-d:dlsPath=<file>` to embed a different `gm.dls`.

The editing, playback and file modules have built-in self-checks. Run them with, for example,
`nim r src/edit.nim`. The modules with checks are `model`, `edit`, `player`, `smf`, `smm` and
`view`.

`python3 tools/mid2svg.py` _in_.mid _out_.svg draws an exported MIDI file as an SVG piano roll,
coloured by channel, for checking exports.
